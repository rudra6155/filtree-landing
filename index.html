The problem

Most trees planted in India don’t survive their first years.

India invests heavily in reforestation every year — government drives, CSR commitments, school planting days. A majority of those saplings die within the first three years. The reasons are rarely dramatic: wrong species for the local soil and climate, no one assigned to care for them past the photo op, no feedback loop between what was planted and what thrived. Filtree exists to make that first decision — which species, where — a scientific one instead of a hopeful one.

How it works

Seven factors. One recommendation.

Filtree’s recommendation engine weighs seven environmental factors against a database of 30+ native Indian species. Here’s what it reads for every location you give it.

  1. 01

    Climate zone

    Köppen classification mapped to species tolerance ranges

  2. 02

    Soil pH

    Pulled from SoilGrids, matched to species pH preference

  3. 03

    Sunlight hours

    Monthly averages at your coordinates, matched to species need

  4. 04

    Available space

    Pot, balcony, yard, or open ground; each species scored for footprint

  5. 05

    Air-quality goal

    PM2.5 absorption rate per species’ leaf surface area

  6. 06

    Water availability

    Annual rainfall plus user watering commitment

  7. 07

    User preference

    Fruit-bearing, flowering, medicinal, sacred, low-maintenance

Every recommendation shows which factors it optimized for, and which it compromised on. No magic picks.

The first garden

The app read his location, surveyed his balcony’s sun hours and railing space, and returned a short list of species suited to a south-facing high-rise balcony with inconsistent watering.
